📚 Learn Zone

Explore the concepts and skills you need to understand and perform fundamental movements safely and effectively.

🎯 Objectives

At the end of the learning experience, the learners should be able to:

  • Cognitive: Identify and explain fundamental movement concepts, including relationships with people, objects, directions, pathways, and available space.
  • Psychomotor: Perform locomotor and manipulative skills safely and demonstrate agility, balance, coordination, and speed during physical activities.
  • Affective: Participate actively, safely, and cooperatively by following rules, respecting shared space, and making responsible movement choices.

1. Explain Fundamental Movement Concepts

Objective: Identify and explain basic movement concepts, including relationships with people, objects, and space.

Meaning: Learners understand how their bodies move in relation to other people, objects, pathways, directions, and available space. They learn to notice where classmates, partners, cones, balls, targets, and obstacles are located and use that information to decide how to move safely and effectively. They also learn that direction, speed, pathway, level, and space can change the way a movement is performed. Understanding these concepts helps learners choose appropriate movements instead of moving without considering what is happening around them.

2. Perform Locomotor Skills Safely

Objective: Perform locomotor skills such as walking, running, hopping, and jumping while avoiding objects or obstacles.

Meaning: Learners demonstrate control while traveling from one place to another and adjust their movement when an obstacle is present. They learn to change direction, speed, pathway, or type of locomotor movement when necessary while staying aware of their own space and the people around them. Safe locomotor movement is not only about moving quickly; it also requires looking ahead, controlling the body, keeping enough distance from others, and making appropriate decisions when the pathway changes.

3. Perform Manipulative Skills

Objective: Demonstrate manipulative skills by sending or propelling an object toward an intended area.

Meaning: Learners use actions such as throwing or striking to control the direction of an object and send it toward a target. They learn that successful object control involves body position, timing, coordination, direction, and appropriate force. Learners also need to consider the distance and location of the target before choosing how to perform the movement. Instead of simply sending an object anywhere, they learn to make a controlled movement that matches the purpose of the activity and the space available.

4. Apply Fitness Concepts

Objective: Demonstrate agility, balance, coordination, and speed during physical activities.

🏃 Fundamental Movement Skills

Locomotor Skills

Locomotor movements carry the whole body from one place to another. Examples include walking, running, hopping, and jumping. These skills help learners travel through pathways, reach targets, avoid obstacles, and respond to changing situations. Effective locomotor movement requires control of direction, speed, balance, and pathway rather than simply moving as fast as possible.

Non-Locomotor Skills

Non-locomotor movements are performed mainly without traveling from one place to another. Examples include bending, stretching, twisting, turning, swaying, and balancing. These movements help learners develop body awareness, flexibility, stability, and control. A learner may use a non-locomotor action while preparing for another movement, maintaining balance, or adjusting body position during an activity.

Manipulative or Object-Control Skills

Manipulative skills involve controlling, handling, or sending an object with the hands, feet, or equipment. Throwing and striking are examples of sending an object toward an intended area. Successful object control requires learners to think about timing, force, direction, distance, body position, and coordination. The goal is not simply to move the object, but to control where and how it travels.

Personal and General Space

Personal space is the immediate area around a learner's body. Being aware of personal space helps prevent unwanted contact and gives the learner enough room to perform movements safely. General space is the larger shared area where several learners move at the same time. In general space, learners must observe classmates, equipment, targets, and obstacles and adjust their direction, speed, and pathway so that everyone can move safely.

Movement Concepts

Direction describes where a person or object moves, such as forward, backward, sideways, upward, downward, or diagonally. Speed refers to how fast or slow a movement is performed and may change depending on the goal, distance, or safety of the activity. Pathway is the route taken by the body or object, such as a straight, curved, or zigzag pathway. Levels describe how high or low the body moves, such as low, medium, or high. Relationships describe how the body or movement is connected to people, objects, or different body parts. Space is the area in which movement takes place and must be considered when choosing where and how to move.

Relationship with People and Objects

Learners need to notice the position and movement of people and objects around them. A classmate may move into the learner's pathway, a cone may block a direct route, or a ball may approach from a different direction. In each situation, the learner can adjust direction, pathway, speed, body position, or distance. This awareness allows movement to become more controlled, purposeful, and safe instead of being performed without considering the surroundings.

Fitness Concepts

Agility is the ability to change body position or direction efficiently. Balance is the ability to maintain stability and body control while still or moving. Coordination allows different body parts to work together smoothly and at the appropriate time. Speed is the ability to perform a movement quickly while maintaining control. These concepts can work together during activities that require a learner to move around an obstacle, keep balance, coordinate the body, and then perform another skill.

Moderate-to-Vigorous Physical Activity (MVPA)

Moderate-to-vigorous physical activity involves movement that requires noticeable physical effort. The level of effort can change depending on the movement, pace, duration, and intensity of the activity. During active games, running, jumping, and other challenging movements may increase the amount of effort needed. Learners should still focus on safe participation, proper instructions, and awareness of their surroundings while being physically active.

Target-Game Concepts

Positioning means choosing a useful location based on the target, people, space, obstacles, and the object being used. People and location in space requires awareness of where other learners are and how their positions affect movement. Relationship to objects refers to the learner's body position in relation to a ball, beanbag, cone, marker, or target. Sending away means propelling an object toward an intended area using appropriate direction, force, timing, and body position. A throw sends an object with the hand or hands, while a strike sends an object away by making controlled contact with a body part or equipment.

Why Fundamental Movement Skills Matter

Fundamental movement skills provide important building blocks for physical activity and many later sports or recreational activities. They help learners develop body control, coordination, balance, spatial awareness, confidence, and the ability to respond to changing movement situations. These skills also support safe participation because learners become more aware of their own bodies, other people, equipment, and available space. When learners practice these skills through enjoyable activities, they can become more prepared to participate actively and cooperatively in different physical activities.
